1769. William Washington Humphries 1 (Martha Elizabeth Gregory , Elizabeth (Betsey) Pegram , Edward , Edward , Daniel , George ) was born on 20 Jun 1841 in Columbus, Lowndes Co., Mississippi. He died on 6 Feb 1904 in Columbus, Lowndes Co., Mississippi.

W W Humphries, Jr. graduated from the University of North Carolina in 1858, and in 1860 he obtained a law degree from Cumberland Law School in Lebanon Tennessee.

He was barely 20 when the War Between the States broke out. He served in Company H of the 35th Miss Volunteer Infantry and rose to the rank of Captain. At one point he was captured but made good his escape by seizing his captors saber when the Yankee officer stuck it in the ground to get a drink alongside a stream. The saber is a treasured family heirloom to this date.

After the war he and General E T Sykes formed the law partership of Humphries & Sykes in Columbus and both prospered. In 1880 the Capt purchased the stately ante-beUum mansion Riverview-- which was featured by LIfe Magazine in its 1945 series "Life Goes To a Party". In 1880 he was also elected to the Miss. State Legislature and served a 4 year term. According to the History of Mississippi by Ethridge, the Legislature almost refused him a seat when it became known that he had fought in a duel, but the seat was proffered when he proved the duel took place in another State.

Quoting Dr. W. L. Lipscomb from his 1909 book A History of Columbus in the 19th Century, "... Capt W. W. Humphries represents his father's intellect and energy- in the army, at the bar, on the legislative floor-- wherever his native town and state has needed a man to defend their honor and advance their interest, he has been ever ready to expend his money, time and talent in their behalf." In a 1905 letter-to-the -editor of the Columbus Commercial Dispatch, one of the Captain's former subordinates lauded the compassion which Capt. Humphries held for troops under his command, and for his vain attempt as Port Commander at Vicksburg to defend the port with only sick and lame troops. The lengthy letter ended "Peace to thee, Capt Humphries; may the sod rest lightly on the form which held thy restless spirit".

William married 1 (1) Fanny Randall (Shanny) Moore daughter of Maj. Moore and Ann Oliver on 11 Jun 1886. Fanny was born on 10 Jun 1859 in Dearbrook Mississippi. She died on 6 Dec 1936 in Columbus, Lowndes Co., Mississippi.

She was a devout Presbyterian, and along with her sister Annie and a few other church ladies, initiated a program of care giving to destitute children; their program evolved into Palmer Home for Children in Columbus.
