2778. Julia Ann Pegram 1 (Roger P. , George Scott , George , George , Daniel , George ) was born 2, 3 on 14 Sep 1844 in Davidson Co., Tennessee. She died in 1923 in Cheatham Co., Tennessee. She was buried in Hutton family cemetery, Davidson Co., Tennessee.

In 1920 she was living with her daughter, Annie L. Cox and her husband James Rufus Cox.

Julia married Dr. William Carter Hutton 1, 2 son of William Drennan Hutton and Virginia Ferebee on 8 Jul 1862. William was born on 20 Jan 1842 in Davidson Co., Tennessee. He died on 8 Feb 1896 in Davidson Co., Tennessee. He was buried in Hutton family cemetery, Davidson Co., Tennessee.

William Carter Hutton was the son of William Drennan Hutton, a farmer and saddle maker. William Drennan Hutton was the son of John Hutton who was born in County Tyrone, Ireland and came to America soon after the Revolution. In 1807 the family of John Hutton moved to Rutherford County, Tennessee although the elder died in Williamson County. William Drennan Hutton settled in the old 14th district of Davidson County. On August 31, 1830 he married Miss Hannah Dellahunty. There were no children born to this marriage. On Oct. 17, 1831 William Hutton bought acreage on the Big Harpeth River from Robert Exum. A few years later he took a second wife, Virginia Ferebee, daughter of Thomas and Sally (Poyner) of Norfolk, Virginia. Virginia Ferebee came to Tennessee in 1819 with her family from Virginia.

William and Virginia Hutton bought additional tracts in 1834 from Moses Patterson on the Harpeth River. Even as late as 1843 they bought 400 acres from Wash Shelton, in the same area of their farm. During their residence at Newsom Station the Huttons had four children born to them: Sally E. Hutton who married (1) James E. Newsom, (2) W. D. Cherry; John H. Hutton who married Margaret Hows; Thomas and ___ Hutton who married (1) E. P. ____; and (2) Martha Ann _____; and William Carter Hutton who married Julia Ann Pegram.

The youngest son, Dr. William Carter Hutton, attended Shelby Medical College and graduated in 1861. His practice was at Pegram, Tennessee. At this settlement he married Julia Pegram, daughter are Roger and Caroline (Williams) Pegram, and the granddaughter of George Scott Pegram, Jr. for whom the town was named. They made their home on Buffalo Road at the present home of Mr. and Mrs. Doug Underwood. It was then called Mount Airy.

During the Civil War he enlisted in Co. A., Rock City Guards, of Nashville, and was mustered into the Confederate Army. He served under General Lee in Northwest Virginia, and was transferred to Stonewall Jackson's Corps. After 12 months service he returned to Tennessee and began his medical practice.

The children of William and Julia Hutton were: Cora V. C. Hutton who married Gustaven C. Thompson; Roger Pegram Hutton who married Sally Newsom Dozier; Willie D. Hutton who married Wilson Thompson, Jr.; Sallie Hutton who married Albert Cox, Jr.; Annie L.. Hutton who married James Rufus Cox; Irving Hutton who married Fannie Newsom; and Dr. Vernon Hutton who married Mamie Cochran.

His obituary from Fulton Daily Leader, 26 April 1917 reads as follows:

Mr. Frank V. Pegram died this morning (Thursday), April 26, 1917, at 9:30 o'clock at the home of his daughter, Mrs. J. B. Cequin, 504 Walnut Street. Mr. Pegram had been enjoying good health until about 10 days ago, when he was taken ill. His condition was not regarded as serious until late hour last evening, when he suddenly grew worse, death following within hours.

The deceased was born in Weakley County, Tennessee, June 8, 1838. In the month of October 1864 he was united in marriage to Miss Elizabeth Williams and in December 1873 moved with thier family to this city.

Mr.Pegram was an honorable, upright Chrsitain gentleman and a faithful attendent both at Sunday school and services at the First Methodist Church. he was an ex-Confederate soldier, belonging to the Fifth Tennessee Regiment and a member of the Camp Jim Pirtle of Fulton.

Surviving are his aged wife and three daughters Mrs. Effie Wittie, Mrs. J. B. Cequin and Miss Ola Pegram, all of whom were present when the end came, one son Herman Pegram, having died a few years ago.

from
History of Audrain County
published 1884, St. Louis, Missouri


farmer and stock raiser. Among the men whose industry and intelligence have been largely influential in making this section of the State what it is--one of the most prosperous in the country, stands prominently the subject of the present sketch, Mr. W. W. Butts. Now approaching middle age, his whole life has been spent in nonest labor as a producer. Nor have his exertions been without success. Mr. Butts has a fine farm of 320 acres, principally the fruit of his own toil, and this is but an emblement of what he has produced as an energetic, enterprising farmer. The agricultural classes are the mainstay and chief source of all material prosperity, and if credit is due to any one for leading a useful and successful life, it is to the farmer upon whom everything edepends. Mr. Butts is a worthy representative of that class of citizens in Audrain county. From his farm, beyond what is needed for home consumption, he is a large producer, sending to the markets every year important yields of both grain and stock which enter in the commerce and business of the country. Like most of the citizens of this section of theState who come of its early families, Mr. Butts came originally from a Southern family. His father, Wilson Butts, was a North Carolinian by nativity, but early went to Tennessee, where later along he was married to Miss Louisa Kingston. In about 1880 they removed to Pike county, Missouri, where the father entered a large body of land. He opened a farm there and followed farming with success until his death, which occurred in about 1851. He was a man of sterling integrity, great energy and more than ordinary public spirit, and was as much esteemed by those who knew him as any man who ever lived this county. Wilson W. was born after the removal of the family to Pike county, on his father’s farm in that county, on the 18th of October, 1841. Reared on the farm, he was educated in the common schools, and on reaching manhood began farming for himself. On the 15th of September, 1870, he was married to Miss Eveline, a daughter of Glen n Pegram, formerly of Tennessee. Mr. Butts continued farming in Pike county until 1877, when he removed to Audrain county, and here bought the land composing his present farm. He improved his own place and has put it in excellent condition. His farm contains good fields and pastures, has good buildings including a new barn just erected, and also an excellent orchard. Mr. Butts is still comparatively a young man and considering his age is more than ordinarily well and comfortably fixed. Although he has resided here note ten years yet he is well and favorably known in the north-eastern part of the county, and is looked upon as one of the most energetic farmers and worthy citizens of Cuivre township. Mr. and Mrs. Butts have had seven children: Homer L., Herman R, Edward K., Ella M., Charles and Elma E. Leslie, the third, died in his second year. Mr. and Mrs. B. are members of the M. E. Church South, and Mr. B. is a member of the Knights of Honor.
