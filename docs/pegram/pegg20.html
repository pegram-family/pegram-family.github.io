264. Ann F. Pegram 1 (George , George , Daniel , Daniel , George ) was born 2 in 1825 in Warren Co., North Carolina.

In the partition of the Estate of her father, George Pegram in 1836, Ann F. Pegram was awarded her share, referred to in the probate records as share 6. She was then a minor and later tried to get an accounting for her share as evidenced by subsequent court records, i.e.:

Aug., Ct., 1841. Drake renewed his bond as gdn. of Ann F. and Eliza Pegram;
Aug. Ct., 1841. Drake filed a gdn. account for all three wards;
Aug. Ct., 1841. Gdn. accounts of Ann F. and Eliza J. Pegram ordered to be audited;
August Court - 1842: Accounts ordered settled of E. D. Drake as gdn. of James J., Ann F. and Eliza J. Pegram; and
Aug. Ct., 1842 Harvey M. Bobbitt and wife brought suit against Edwin D. Drake, Matthew M. Drake and Nathan Milam for debts.

Note: While Ann is not named as the wife of Harvey M. Bobbitt in the suit against Edwin D. Drake, et al, there seems little doubt as to her identity based on a deed executed by Ann Bobbitt in the partition of the estate of her great-aunts in 1856 as shown below.

By deed dated 4 Oct 1856 Ann Bobbitt to John L.. Pegram for $8 - her right to 126 acre tract adjoining lands of William Pegram, Joseph Riggan, Martha Pegram and other - tract known as the Sarah & Lucretia Pegram tract. Wit: Wm. C. Clanton, Peter E. Robertson (jurat) Proved Nov Ct. 1856, Reg. 10 Feb. 1857, (Warren Co., North Carolina DB31:617)

In 1860, Ann is listed as HH. The only children in the home are Josephine (shown as J. H. age 12) and a young daughter, listed as L. Bobbitt, age 1.

Ann married Harvey (Harry) M. Bobbitt 1 son of William Bobbitt and Winifred "Winny" Bennett on 9 Mar 1842 in Warren Co., North Carolina. Harvey was born in 1820 in Warren Co., North Carolina. He died before 1860. Harvey was counted in a census in 1850 in Warren Co., North Carolina.

The 1850 Census has Harvy indexed as Harry Bobbitt. He is age 30 and Ann is age 25. They have 3 daughters: Elizabeth, age 6, F___ age 4 and Josephine, age 2. Either this record or his Civil War record is in error.

He enlisted in on 10 June 1862 in Richmond, Virginia when his age was given as 46. He became a POW and was confined at Point Lookout, Maryland in Oct. 1864. He was exchanged at Camp Lee, Virginia on 26 January 1865.

They had the following children:

  1146 F i Elizabeth Bobbitt was born about 1844 in Warren Co., North Carolina.

1148 F iii Josephine H. Bobbitt was born about 1848.

The following is from Henderson, Archibald, North Carolina, the Old North State and the New; Chicago: Lewis Publishing Co., 1941:

Henry Andrew Battle, who resides two and one-half miles northeast of Red Oak, on the highway between Halifax and Nashville, has always devoted his attention to agricultural pursuits since attaining his majority. He was born In Nash County, near Whitakers, May 25, 1866, and is a son of Lawrence and Elizabeth(Taylor) Battle, also natives of Nash County. The father was born near Hilliardston in October, 1834, and the mother near Whitakers in 1833. Lawrence Battle acquired his education in an academy in Lewiston and engaged in agricultural pursuits In Nash County until a few years prior to his death in 1912. He always voted with the Democratic party and filled the office of justice of the peace. His religious belief was indicated in his consistent membership in the Baptist church. To him and his wife were born five children : Mary, the widow of James Exum, of Spartanburg, South Carolina; Tempe Perry, the wife of Samuel S. Alsop, of Enfield, Halifax County, North Carolina; John Wilkes Booth, who died in 1915; Henry Andrew, of this review; and Elizabeth Frances, who became the wife of Allan Whitaker and who is now deceased.

After attending the public schools of Nash County, Henry A. Battle took up farming, which he has since followed, occupying a pleasant home two and one-half miles northeast of Red Oak. He produces the crops best adapted to soil and climatic conditions here and employs progressive methods in the cultivation of his land. His political allegiance Is given to the Democratic party.

On the 4th of March, 1908, Mr. Battle was united in marriage to Miss Lucy Hilliard Vaughan, daughter of James Madison and Mourning Boddie (Hilliard) Vaughan, of Red Oak township, Nash County, North Carolina. Her father was born in Warren County, North Carolina, November 7, 1817, and was a son of Peyton Vaughan who was born in Greensville County, Virginia, and there pursued his education. After attaining his majority he removed to Warren County, North Carolina, where he taught school, particularly higher mathematics. His son, James Madison Vaughan, obtained his education in public and private schools and devoted his life to farming in Nash County, where he died November 4, 1886. He served in the Civil War, being on duty at Camp Holmes, near Raleigh, North Carolina. He always supported the Democratic party and he belonged to the Methodist Episcopal church, in which he served on the board of stewards and was also a teacher in the Sunday school. He wedded Mourning Boddie Hilliard, of Red Oak township, Nash County. She was born December 21, 1836, in Nash County where Mr. and Mrs. Battle now reside, and she acquired her education under private tutors at home and in a private school in Warrenton. She died July 29, 1917. To Mr. and Mrs. Vaughan were horn five children : Mary Anne, who is deceased ; Lucy Hilliard, the wife of Henry Andrew Battle; Tempe William, who became the wife of Cicero F. Ellen and who has passed away; Olivia Celina, deceased; and Samuel Johnson, who has also departed this life.

In tracing the ancestry of Mrs. Battle it is learned that her great-great-grandfather was Isaac Hilliard, who was born in 1738 and died in 1790. He lived on the Woodlawn Plantation in Nash County and served as an officer in the Revolutionary War. He was educated in the College of William and Mary and in 1765 he married Leah Crawford, who was born in 1743 In Surry County, Virginia, and died in 1826. James Hilliard, the great-grandfather of Mrs. Battle, was born in 1768 and was educated by English tutors, by tutors from the North and by attending a northern college. He founded Hilliiardston Academy at Hllliardston, North Carolina. His wife was Mourning Boddie. Isaac Hilliard fought at the battle of Guilford Court House In the Revolutionary War while serving as a captain of infantry. The grandfather of Mrs. Battle in the maternal line was James Crawford Hilliard, who was born in 1806 and died in 1860. He was educated at the University of Virginia and became a planter.
