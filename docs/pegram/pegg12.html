148. Winchester Pegram 1, 2 (Daniel , Edward , Daniel , George ) was born on 23 Jan 1799 in Dinwiddie Co., Virginia. He died on 28 Nov 1862 in Dallas, Gaston Co., North Carolina.

Winchester and Mary lived near the mouth of the South Fork River in Lincoln County, which is now known as the South Point Section of Gaston County, having been cut off from Lincoln County in 1846. This area was also the general locale of Daniel Pegram, Winchester's father.

About 1850 Winchester and Mary moved to Dallas, just north of Gastonia. Winchester was a merchant there, and also kept a hotel. The Gastonia, North Carolina Daily Gazette of 28 September 1945 carried an article entitled "Old Pegram Home, First built in Dallas, Being Tom Down by Workmen." It was a large two story house with an ell, and chimney at either end. It was on the southwest comer of the square. It was built by Col. Morris, almost all of heart pine. It was a stately building and stood for ninety five years before being torn down to be replaced by an oil station. The home is described in a book on Old Gaston County Homes. Miss Corinne Puett, a descendant of Winchester and Mary Stowe, wrote on page four of"Dallas Homes" that "Mr. Pegram was killed by a falling tree in 1861, and Mrs. Pegram continued to live there until her death in 1866. Mrs. Pegram had a pretty flower garden on the west side of her house, and today many of Grandma Pegram's flower descendants live on in the gardens of her children and grandchildren."A daughter, Mary Ward made her home with her mother, and continued to live there a long time after her mother died. Later the home was occupied by Sheriff Jim White, and Mrs. O.F. Mason and family. Mrs. Miles Rhyne bought the home, and later her son, Eugene Rhyne owned it, and lived there for many years. Dr. Simmons visited Miss Puett in the summer of 1982. She was in her nineties, in the poorest of health, and confined to the bed, but still interested and alert about family history. She has since died.

The Pegrams were charter members of the Presbyterian Church at Dallas, which was organized in 1851. Winchester was the first Ruling Elder. His descendants are still leaders in the Presbyterian Denomination. One of his grandsons, Robert Murry Pegram was a Presbyterian minister, and there were one or more other descendants that were ministers. Mrs. Frances Pegram Weaver and her two sisters, great granddaughters of Winchester and Mary Pegram were active in Presbyterian work, have held responsible appointments, and have been officially honored for their contributions.

The death of Winchester Pegram resulted when he tried to rescue a calf from underneath a falling tree. In the Annals of Lincoln County, page 173 it is stated: "Winchester Pegram was a leading citizen.., born June 23, 1799. He married in 1818 to Mary, daughter of Jacob Stowe, and died in Dallas November 28, 1862. He was a good man, a merchant, kept a hotel in Dallas and reared a family of splendid sons and daughters, among them were Miles P., longtime a Charlotte Banker, W.W., E.L. Theodore and Frank Pegram."

Both Winchester and his wife Mary are buried at the New Hope Presbyterian Church Cemetery in Gaston County, North Carolina.

The will of Winchester was executed in Gaston County, 5 August 1857 and recorded in Book 1, p. 134. It names his wife Mary, and daughters, Violet Susan Froneberger and Mary Ward. Daughters Nancy and Elvira died prior to the time Winchester's will was made. Sons named in the will were Miles P., Theodore, William, Edward L. and Franklin. It also named Rebecca and Violet Craig, daughter of his deceased daughter Nancy. The will of Mary Stowe Pegram was made 10 March 1866, Book 1, p. 193, and names the same heirs as Winchester.

Winchester and Mary had a large family and there are many descendants in North and South Carolina, especially the area around Charlotte and Gastonia, and in York County, South Carolina. All of Winchester and Mary's children were apparently born in Gaston or Lincoln Counties.

A copy of a portion of the Bible of Daniel Theodore Pegram, son of Winchester Pegram is posted elsewhere on this website.

156. Thomas Rives (Thomas Henry Rives , Mary Pegram , Daniel , George ) was born about 1770 in Dinwiddie Co., Virginia. He died in 1842 in Hall Co., Georgia.

After the custom of the time, Thomas Rives had received land from his father before the latter's death. As early as 1810 the records show him as the owner of 10 slaves in Chatham county.

In 1811, following the death of his father, Thomas Rives removed with his family to Jones County, Georgia, and settled six miles from the Ocmulgee River which, at that time, was the dividing line between the Creek Indians and the whites. In 1817 he removed to Jackson County, Georgia, and settled three miles north of Jefferson; but remained there only a short while, finally making his home near Bark Camp in Hall County, Georgia. The date of his death is not known; his great-great-grandson, Col. Homer V. Johnson, Clerk of the Court of Hall county, having indicated that there is no record of his will or of the administration of his estate among the Hall county records.

Thomas married Susanna Thweatt daughter of Buwell Thweatt and Jane Manson in 1793 in Mecklenburg Co., Virginia. Susanna was born in 1775 in Dinwiddie Co., Virginia. She died in 1833 in Hall Co., Georgia.

158. Mary Rives (Thomas Henry Rives , Mary Pegram , Daniel , George ) was born about 1774 in Virginia.

Mary married Robert Wilkinson son of John Wilkinson and Margaret McGume before 1790 in Chatham Co., North Carolina. Robert was born in 1764 in Orange Co., North Carolina.

The following note (and all other notes on this Wilkinson line) was made by the unknown researcher who compiled this excellent account of his/her Wilkinson genealogy. If you recognize the author of this work, please let us hear from you so that we may give proper recognition for their excellent work.

"Robert Wilkinson, the first son of John and Margaret Wilkinson, was born in Chatham County, North Carolina in 1764. According to a book of Rives family history, Robert married Mary Rives. Mary was the daughter of Thomas Henry Rives and second wife Mary Edwards. She was born in 1768. Thomas was a son of William Rives and Miss Pegram, born in Prince George County, Virginia in about 1740. Thomas had moved to southern Virginia and lived in that area until about 1768. After the death of his first wife, Eleanor Neal, in 1868, he married Mary Edwards. He and family moved a few years later to Chatham County, North Carolina.

In the 1790 Census of Chatham County, Robert and Mary were known to have three daughters and two sons. I believe that one of the sons was Benjamin. By 1800 the family had added another daughter and son. The family remained in Chatham County for at least the next 20 years. It is believed that Mary was deceased by about 1818. In 1818, Robert appointed Thomas Snipes as his Power of Attorney to act for him for sums of money or property due him from the Last Will and Testament of Thomas Rives. The nature of the Power of Attorney indicated that Robert was considering leaving Chatham County. He is believed to have been living there until about 1823, after which there is no further record of him or any members of his family in North Carolina.

The birth of my known ancestor, Robert (1797), fits into the picture very nicely. As was the practice in many families of the time, the first born son was often named after the father. A review of other children born to Robert Sr.'s brothers and sisters reveals no other Robert as a child. While all of this is circumstantial, it is too good not to be true."
