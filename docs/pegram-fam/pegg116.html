2517. Henrietta Rives (James Franklin Rives , Edwards Rives , Thomas Henry Rives , Mary Pegram , Daniel , George ) was born 1, 2 in 1842 in Chatham Co., North Carolina. She died on 20 Jun 1895 in Charlotte, Mecklenburg Co., North Carolina.

Henrietta married James Cornelius Dowd son of Willis Dickerson Dowd and Ann Mariah Gaines. James was born 1, 2 in 1836 in Concord, Cabarrus Co., North Carolina. He died in 1895.

The following is from a report prepared by: Dr. Dan L. Morrill, Director Charlotte-Mecklenburg Historic Properties Commission regarding the Dowd home in Charlotte,

"On December 30, 1878, James C. Dowd (1835-1898) purchased approximately 250 acres of land from his brother, Clement Dowd, just to the west of the City of Charlotte. A native of Moore County, North Carolina and a son of Confederate General Willis D. Dowd, James C. Dowd had served as a captain in the North Carolina 45th Regiment during the Civil War and had been wounded at the Battle of Fredericksburg. During the decade following that conflict, he had migrated to Mecklenburg County with his first wife, Henrietta Rives. He erected the James C. Dowd House c. 1879. By the time of his death on November 26, 1898, Mr. Dowd had become "One of the most prominent farmers in the county." It was fitting that his funeral was held at Tryon St. Baptist Church (later First Baptist Church), because he had served as a deacon in that religious organization for many years.

Henrietta Rives Dowd, a native of Chatham County, North Carolina, predeceased her husband, having expired on June 20, 1895, at the age of fifty-two. She had given birth to ten children who attained adulthood, four girls and six boys. Among them were William Carey Dowd, who bought The Charlotte News in 1895, and Willis Frank Dowd, who established the Charlotte Pipe and Foundry Co. in 1900. Mr. Dowd's second wife, Alice Livingston of Huntersville, North Carolina, died in January 1900. "

While family sources show he was born in Cabarrus Co., North Carolina, in 1850 he is found with his family in Moore Co., North Carolina.

2534. Frances (Fannie) Harris Headen 1 (Mary Edwards Rives , Edwards Rives , Thomas Henry Rives , Mary Pegram , Daniel , George ) was born 2 in Sep 1864 in Chatham Co., North Carolina. She died 3 on 7 Feb 1917 in Chapel Hill, Orange Co., North Carolina.

Fannie and their daughter, Mary Louise, died in the Great Flu Epidemic. R. L. could no longer live at "Plum Nelly", so he built a substantial house, current 741 East Franklin Street, into which he and his youngest daughter, Elizabeth, relocated. It had the first central heat in town, complete with a control button at the the head of R. L.'s bed. The street, which winds out of Chapel Hill in the direction of Durham, was once known as "Strowd Hill". The house is in good repair. In the early 1920's he traveled by train from North Carolina down into the deep southern states and then across to Texas. He sent articles to the Chapel Hill newspaper describing the vastness of the King Ranch and of seeing the Mexican cowboys roping the fat cattle, and of crossing the Rio Grande into Matamoras, Mexico, where he saw humble homes, a pig standing in one doorway, "with as much dignity as if it were a maid or a butler". He sent a photograph of himself holding a rifle and a string of freshly shot ducks. He was wearing a suit, a white standup starched collar, and a hat. They say that in Chapel Hill townsfolk could set their watches by his 4:PM rides through the village, a chauffer at the wheel. R. L. was a Methodist, a Mason, and an Elk.

Frances married 4 Robert Lee Strowd 1, 2, 3 son of William Franklin Strowd and Lois (Laura) Atwater on 27 Apr 1886 in North Carolina. Robert was born in Feb 1864 in Chapel Hill, Orange Co., North Carolina. He died in 1934 in Chapel Hill, Orange Co., North Carolina.

The 1900 Census of Orange Co., North Carolina shows him listed as Robert R. Stroud. Living with him are his mother and father. The census shows his parents have been married for 39 years. They have had 2 children with 1 living. Robert and Fannie have been married for 14 years and have had 5 children, all living.

Robert Lee Strowd was the only survivng child of Buck and Lou Strowd. He and his wife, Fannie Harris Headen lived on 1,000 acres which R. L.'s father had given them. The old house is still standing, once called "Plum Nelly" because it was "plum" out of Chapel Hill and nearly to Durham, surrounded by houses. It once had a handsome gazebo on the back lawn. The area has been known as Davie Circle for some time. R. L. was a merchant in Chapel Hill, occupying his own building which still bears the name The Strowd Building on the brickwork. He was Vice president of the Bank of Chapel Hill and also postmaster for a while.
