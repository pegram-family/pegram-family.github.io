1069. Rev. Thomas Howard Pegram (Jacob , John , George , Daniel , Daniel , George ) was born 1, 2, 3 on 11 Jan 1825 in Guilford Co., North Carolina. He died on 24 Mar 1906 in Winston Salem, Forsyth Co., North Carolina. He was buried in Salem Cemetery..

His obituary which appeared in the Washington Post on March 25, 1906 reads as follows:

"Winston-Salem, N. C., March 24. Rev. Thomas Howard Pegram, one of the most widely-known ministers in North Carolina, died here to-day in his eighty-second year. He was born January 11, 1825, in Guilford County, and was licensed to exhort at the age of nineteen years. He was licensed to preach and joined the North Carolina Methodist Protestant Conference .in 1847. He filled the highest office in the gift of that body, being elected president two or three times. He joined the Methodist Episcopal Church at the annual session of the North Carolina Conference held at Salisbury In 1877. At his request he was superannuated at the annual session of the Western North Carolina M. E. Conference at High Point in 1904. During his unbroken ministry of more than fifty years ho built thirty-seven churches and several parsonages.

Before his death he planned his own funeral, which will be held Monday. Six ministers were chosen by him for active pallbearers."

Thomas married Elizabeth Tyson Jones daughter of Caleb Callus Jones and Delaney Medaris on 11 Jan 1847. Elizabeth was born 1 on 22 Jun 1826 in Forsyth Co., North Carolina. She died on 17 Apr 1889 in Forsyth Co., North Carolina.

1072. John M. Pegram (Jacob , John , George , Daniel , Daniel , George ) was born 1, 2, 3 on 24 Dec 1830 in North Carolina.

The following is from William G. Cutler's History of the State of Kansas - 1883:

J. M. Pegram, miller and farmer, Section 33, P. O. Mulvane, was born in North Carolina in 1829, son of Jacob and Ursula (Dwiggins) Pegram. He married, in 1851, Miss Lydia Stephens, daughter of George and Sarah Stephens, born in North Carolina in 1810. They have one child, Albert. Mr. Pegram came to Kansas in 1875, located in Belle Plaine, Sumner County, and engaged in farming until 1878, when he moved to Mulvane and engaged in merchandising. In 1882 he moved to Salem Township, and purchased the flouring mill on the Cow Skin Creek, Section 33; mill 28x30 feet, two stories, known as the Mulvane Mill; has two run of stone, with a capacity of 100 bushels per day. He was elected Justice of the Peace in Mulvane in 1881; resigned in the fall of the same year, on account of other business. He is a member of the Society of Friends. Left North Carolina in 1861, on account of persecution, he being a strong Anti-slavery man.

John married 1 Lydia Stephens daughter of George Stephens and Sarah Unknown on 19 Aug 1850. Lydia was born 2, 3, 4 in 1811 in North Carolina.
