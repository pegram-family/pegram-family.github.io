59. William Pegram (George , Daniel , Daniel , George ) was born 1, 2 about 1778 in Warren Co., North Carolina. He died about 1876 in Warren Co., North Carolina.

Shirley Cahoon has provided us with a copy of the administration of the Estate of William Pegram. In May Court of 1877 in Warren Co., North Carolina we have the following:

William G. Egerton, Administrator of William Pegram, Dec. states that upon the death of William Pegram his Estate descended "to his children Allen Pegram, Emily Pegram, Martha Harris, N.P. Harris and Brack, widow of George Brack, all of whom are of full age and residents of said Co., of Warren." We can not explain why the grand-children of deceased parents are not included as devisees.

N. P. Harris is Nancy P. who married Newsom Harris.

We are still unsure about the spouse of Martha (Patsy) Harris but if she was still unmarried at age 42, she probably left no children. The 1850 Census shows:

William Pegram - 72
Nancy Pegram - 63
Martha Pegram - 42
Emeline Pegram - 23
Elizabeth Pegram - 21
Priestly Harris - 15

The 1860 Census for Warren Co., p. 580, shows:

W. Pegram - 85
N. Pegram - 85
E. Pegram - 30

The leaves us with 2 possibilities. Either Martha married between 1850 and 1860 or she is listed wrong on the 1850 Census.

If the 1850 ceneus is correct, then Martha (Patsy) is still single and living at home. We have found no marriage bond in Warren Co. for a Martha or Patsy Pegram to a Harris after the 1850 census that we can identify as this Martha although she is identified as Martha Harriss in her father's probate records and shown as Patsy Harriss on the 1880 Census.

Emily PEGRAM Self F S W 50 NC
Patsy HARRISS Sister F W W 68 NC
Elizabeth BRACK Sister F W W 48 NC
Delia BRACK Niece F S W 19 NC

We have not yet identified William on the 1870 Census. We know he was still alive but we can not find him on the 1870 Census of Warren Co.

Note that we have also listed Mildred as a daughter of William. Mildred has previously been shown as a daughter of Henry Pegram. However, based on the 1810 and 1820 Census records of Warren Co., Henry only had 2 daughters. Also, we have been furnished with a report prepared for Robert Boykin which shows Mildred to be a daughter of William. There is no question that the Priestly (Presley) Harris shown living with William and Nancy on the 1850 Census is a son of Mildred and William F. Harris. The younger children of William F. Harris and Mildred Pegram Harris are found living with their father and his new wife in 1850 but Presley is living with his grandfather.

62. Nancy Pegram (George , Daniel , Daniel , George ) was born about 1784. She died 1 before 1850 in Robertson Co., Tennessee.

From the Will of George Pegram, Sr. we know that he had one grandson named Micager Bell. Based on Deed dated 24 Feb 1857 wherein John W. Riggan, Agt. for Geo. W. Bell,, Rebecca Bell & Caroline Bell to John L. Pegram for $23 conveyed their undivided right in the Estate of George's daughters, in 126 acre tract. It is now confirmed that these are children of Nancy Bell and her husband Jacob Bell.

At the same time we find the following: 24 Feb 1857 - John W. Riggan as Agt. for James Moore & Cynthia Moore (his wife) of Chatham Co., TN to John L. Pegram for $7.80 - right & title to 126 acre tract (description as above). Wit. Thom. A. Montgomery, Nathl. Nicholson ( jurat). Proved Feb 1857. Reg. 20 Mar 1857 (DB:31:653). Cynthia Bell married James Moore and she is identified as the daughter of Nancy Bell and Jacob Bell in her father's Will.

Nancy married 1 Jacob Bell son of Jesse Bell and Shearin on 24 Dec 1800 in Warren Co., North Carolina. Jacob was born 2 in 1773 in Warren Co., North Carolina. He died in 1854 in Cheatham Co., Tennessee.

The 1850 Census of Robertson Co., Tennessee shows Jacob Bell, age 77; Rebecca, age 40; Caroline, age 38; Parthenia, age 37; George, age 29. All were born in North Carolina. The part of Robertson Co. where Jacob lived later was incorporated into Cheatham Co. where he later died.

The Will of Jacob Bell dated July 20, 1854 and probated in Cheatham County, Tennessee reads as follows:

I, Jacob Bell to make and publish this as my last Will and Testament hereby revoking and making void all other wills by me at anytime made.

1st I direct that my funeral expenses and all my just debts be paid as soon after my death as possible out of any monies that I may died possessed of, or may first come into the hands of my executor.

2nd I give and bequeath to my son G. W. Bell all my lands except 200 acres where John H. Harper settled at. Also I give to my son G. W. Bell one negro man named Henry, one negro woman named -- -two negro boys, one named Tom the other named Parker, also all my horses except one, also one yoke oxen, one wagon and geer, one ox cart, three shot guns, one case of bottles, all my farming implements, the first choice of my bedstead and furniture, half of my stock of hogs, one silver watch. The crop that may be on hand to be divided equally between my son G. W. Bell and my daughters Rebecca and Caroline Bell.

3d I give and bequeath to my two daughters Rebecca & Caroline Bell 200 acres of land, the place where John H.. Harper settled on, to have as a home during their natural lives, then go to my son forever, and that my son G. W. Bell have the management of the land during their lives, also I give to them all my cattle except the oxen, and the other half of my stock of hogs.

Fourthly I give and bequeath to my daughter, Rebecca Bell six hundred dollars of the money I sold the girl June for, if I do not buy her another negro with the money, also I give to her my second choice of bedsteads and furniture. I also give to my daughters Rebecca and Caroline Bell, one horse.

Fifthly I give and bequeath to my daughter Caroline Bell one negro boy named Jim, one bedstead and furniture one safe and one trunk.

Lastly, it is my wish and desire that my executor should sell every species of property that I may die possessed of except what has been specially willed away and if I should have any money left on hand after paying my debts, I wish it together with the amount of the sale of the above mentioned property equally divided between my son G. W. Bell and my two daughters, Caroline and Rebecca Bell.

Seventhly I give and bequeath to my daughter Cynthia Moore fifty dollars, also one bedstead and furniture and after her death to her son Charles Moore.

Eightly I give and bequeath to my two grandchildren John and Lucy Evans, fifty dollars each.

Ninthly It is my wish and desire that my son G. W. Bell pay out of his part of my estate the three bequests made to my daughter Cynthia Moore and John and Lucy Evans.

Lastly, I nominate and appoint my son G. W. Bell & James J. Williams my executor to this my last Will and Testament.

In testimony whereof I, Jacob Bell do set my hand and seal this date 20th July 1854.

Jacob Bell (Seal)

Signed, Sealed and delivered in the presence of

W. W. Williams
J. D. Nicholson

Jacob Bell married Nancy Pegram, daughter of George Pegram, Sr. of Warren County, North Carolina. Based on this Will, it appears that his son Micajah has predeceased his father. It is obvious his wife Nancy is now deceased which is confirmed by the 1850 Census.

Since she is not named in his Will, it would appear that Parthena may have died between 1850 and 1854.
