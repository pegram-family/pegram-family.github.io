The following is from "North Carolina, the Old North State and the new" by Archibald Henderson, (Chicago: Lewis Publishing Co., 1941)

Eugene Clarence Judd, M. D., numbered among Raleigh's representative surgeons, was born on a farm at Newhill, Wake County, North Carolina, April 24, 1882, his parents being John Thomas and Louise Francis Cross Avent Judd. The ancestral history shows that the family is of English origin and was founded in Plymouth, Massachusetts, in 1632. Later on removal was made to Hartford, Connecticut, and thence to North Carolina, various branches of the family living in Wake County. The genealogical record of the Judds appears in First Families of America. the doctor's father, John Thomas J., who was born in 1843 and died in 1913, was a son of John William and Melinda (Dennis) all of Wake County. He enlisted for service in the last year of the Civil War and he spent his youthful days on the farm in Wake County on which he was born. Following his marriage to Louise Frances Cross Avent he engaged in farming in Chatham County for a few years and then returned to his birthplace in Wake County. He was active in township affairs, was an ardent Democrat and was a candidate for the Legislature in 1898. He failed of election in that year but in 1911 represented his county in the General assembly. He was a man of progressive spirit, interested in all civic improvements. To him and his life or born seven children, as follows: Sarah Louise, the wife of Nathaniel G. Yarborough, of Cary, North Carolina; Lena Williams, the wife of Dr. Benjamin Wise Burt; Dr. James Mahlon Judd, physician, banker and manufacturer of Varina, North Carolina, who is mentioned at length on another page of this work; John Herbert Judd, D.D.S., who is engaged in the practice of dentistry at Fayetteville; William Arthur Judd; Dr. Zebulon Judd, an outstanding educator of the South, who is dean of the school of Education of the Alabama Polytechnic Institute at Auburn, Alabama; and Dr. Eugene Clarence Judd.

The last named spent his youthful days on his father's farm and attended the Cary high school. He taught school for two years, spending one year in the public schools of Wake County and one year in the Buckhorn school of Chatham County. He next became a student in the University of North Carolina, which he attended from 1904 until 1907, and by working on farms in the summer months he largely met the expenses of his college course. he studied medicine at the University of North Carolina from 1907 until 1909. In 1911, he was graduated from the medical Department of the University of Pennsylvania and he became student assistant demonstrator of anatomy. As an interne he was connected with the Rex Hospital of Raleigh and was with the Rockefeller Sanitary Commission in its work in western North Carolina. he began the practice some that a Sundays at you quote Springs, in association with his brother, Dr. James M. Judge, and in 1913 he came to Raleigh were he became associated with Dr. A. H. Royster, one of the foremost surgeons of North Carolina. He served as physician to the North Carolina State Prison under Governor Bickett in 1917 and in the following year resigned to enter the medical department of the United States Navy. He was commissioned a lieutenant, junior grade, in October of that year but because of the influenza epidemic did not get into active service until January 1, 1919, when he took up naval recruiting service in Raleigh. He was a lieutenant, senior grade, when he received his discharge in September, 1919. He has taken special postgraduate work in surgery and since 1919 has engaged in general surgical practice in the capital city. Dr. Judd has served as president all the Wake County Medical Society and president of the Raleigh Academy of Medicine, and he also belongs to the North Carolina State Medical Society and the American Medical Association. He is on the surgical staff all the Rex, Saint Agnes and Mary Elizabeth hospitals of Raleigh and is a past president of the Rex Hospital staff. He has written many papers on surgical subjects. For more than 20 years he has served gratuitously as general surgeon to the Methodist Orphanage in Raleigh and during the entire period there has not been a single fatality among all the orphanage children.

On the 26th of October, 1920, Dr. Judge married Mary Elleanor Pritchard, of Chapel Hill, who was graduated from Elon College with the Bachelor of Arts degree and from the University of North Carolina with the Master of Arts degree. She belongs to the Women's Club, the Medical Auxiliary of Wake County and the American Legion Auxiliary, and for years has been a teacher in the North Carolina School for the Blind at Raleigh. The Doctor belongs to the American Legion and, like his wife, holds membership in the Edenton Street Methodist Church.
